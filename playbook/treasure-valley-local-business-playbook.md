# The Treasure Valley Local-Business Playbook: Drive Traffic & Automate the Sale

> **What this is.** A reusable operating manual for a solo small-business owner in the Boise–Meridian–Eagle–Nampa–Caldwell corridor (Ada & Canyon counties). It is built around a refurbished-designer-sectional resale business (boise-sectionals.com), but every play generalizes to any local product or service business — swap the nouns, keep the skeleton.
>
> **How to read it.** Sections 4–6 are the *why* (market, competitors, demand). Section 7 is the *how* — six playbooks (A local SEO, B paid ads, C organic/community, D checkout, E automation, F metrics). Section 8 sequences them into a 30/60/90-day rollout. Section 9 lets you re-template the whole thing for your own business; Sections 10–11 give you a cost table and the things to verify before spending.
>
> **The core thesis.** The Treasure Valley adds tens of thousands of newcomers a year who have no "my guy" yet — and almost no local SMB lets them actually *buy* without a phone call, text, or showroom visit. In furniture resale specifically, **4 of 4 competitors force a call/text/visit to purchase.** The operator who combines (1) local traffic, (2) a real buy button, and (3) a hands-off automated backend — so "all the customer does is buy" — wins demand the slow incumbents never see.

## Worked example #1 — A duct & vent cleaning business (Before → After)

This is a real local example: a Treasure Valley operator (we'll call him Sam) marketing **residential air-duct & vent cleaning** inside a 208-area Facebook buy/sell/trade group. He's a perfect test of this playbook — he has genuine demand and a real offer, but is running the *fully manual* version of everything. Here's exactly how to put him on the system above.

### Before — what he does today

| Stage | What Sam does now | The leak |
|---|---|---|
| **Traffic** | One heartfelt post in **one** Facebook group, `@everyone` tag, before/after duct photos | Single non-compounding channel. No Google Business Profile, no website, no Nextdoor, no review bank — the post sinks down the feed and the reach is gone |
| **Offer** | "Licensed • Bonded • Insured," before/after proof, **"Pay AFTER the service & satisfaction,"** "Special offer — first 20 customers," scarcity | The offer is genuinely strong — but it's trapped inside a disappearing post and has no posted price |
| **Lead capture** | "Message me your **ZIP code** for a free quote" / "Text 414‑500‑0118" / Messenger `m.me` link | 100% manual. Every lead is a DM he answers by hand and quotes ZIP-by-ZIP. Nights/work hours = missed leads = lost sales (the 5-minute rule, violated by default) |
| **Quote → book → pay** | Manual back-and-forth, then pay after the job | No instant price, no online booking, no calendar, no deposit. He *is* the booking system |
| **Reviews** | A few comments under the post | No flywheel — none of this trust compounds into the next sale |

**Diagnosis:** Sam is doing the *hard* version of both halves — reach that doesn't compound, and a back-end that's 100% him. He maps cleanly onto the **service** track of every playbook below.

### The five concrete moves for Sam (in order)

1. **Stand up a Google Business Profile** as a service-area business (hide address; areas = Meridian, Boise, Eagle, Nampa, Caldwell). This is the free, *compounding* asset his single FB post can never be — it puts him in the Maps 3-pack for "air duct cleaning near me," which ~44% of local clicks go to. *(Playbook A)*
2. **Productize the price.** Replace "message me your ZIP for a quote" with **flat-price packages** — e.g. *Up to 10 vents $X · 11–20 vents $Y · whole-home + dryer vent $Z*. Posted prices are exactly what the winning service competitors (Top Shelf Junk, Gemstar Detailing) do, and they out-review everyone *because* they removed the friction. *(Playbook D, service track)*
3. **Add a "Book Now" page** with a calendar and a deposit at booking (Square Appointments free + Stripe; card on file kills no-shows). Now "all the customer does is buy." His FB posts and GBP both link here instead of to his DMs. *(Playbook D)*
4. **Automate the first reply.** Missed-call-text-back + an AI triage step that asks the two questions he asks by hand (ZIP / home size), returns the matching package price, and drops the booking link — within 60 seconds, 24/7. This single scenario recovers the after-hours leads he loses today. *(Playbook E, Scenario 2 + 3)*
5. **Turn the job into the next lead.** Auto review request +1 day after every clean (he already takes before/after photos — perfect review bait), plus a $/$ referral card at the door. Reviews feed the GBP that feeds the 3-pack. *(Playbook A/C)*

**Why it wins:** Sam keeps everything that's already working — the emotional FB reach, the before/after proof, the strong "pay-after"/guarantee offer — but bolts on the two things he's missing: a **compounding traffic asset** and a **self-serve, automated path to buy.** His competitors in cleaning are still quoting ZIP-by-ZIP in DMs too, so the first one to add a buy button takes the demand the rest never see. That's the whole thesis, applied to one real business.

## Market context: the Treasure Valley opportunity

The Treasure Valley — the Boise metro running west along I-84 through Meridian, Eagle, Nampa, and Caldwell — is one of the fastest-growing regions in the United States. For a local operator, that growth *is* the thesis: a steady inflow of new households who arrive without an established roster of local vendors, many moving into brand-new, empty homes that need furniture, services, and trades.

### Population and growth

- **Meridian (zip 83642)** is Idaho's second-largest city and its fastest-growing — roughly **140,000 in 2024**, up **18.6% from 2020–2024** (71st among all U.S. cities for growth), and estimated near **148,000 in 2026**. Annual growth runs **~3.2–3.5%**. ([BoiseDev](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/05/19/five-idaho-cities-among-nations-fastest-growing/), [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/idaho/meridian))
- **Boise** anchors the metro at ~480,000 in the core; the city itself grew slowly (~1.1%) because it is largely built out — growth spilled to the suburbs. ([Macrotrends](https://www.macrotrends.net/global-metrics/cities/22938/boise-city/population))
- **Nampa** (~125,000 in 2026) is the metro's second-largest city; **Caldwell** (~72,000) and **Eagle** (~33,000) round out the corridor. Boomtowns **Star (+14.7%)** and **Kuna** each grew >10% in a single year. ([World Population Review – Nampa](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/idaho/nampa), [Idaho Press](https://www.idahopress.com/news/these-rural-boomtowns-lead-dramatic-growth-in-treasure-valley/article_32ee9cc5-ad30-4e55-9d3b-2f1c18511966.html))
- **Ada + Canyon counties have grown 20%+ since the 2020 Census**, with the Ada–Canyon core estimated near **847,840 in 2025** — over 40% of Idaho's population — and the valley projected to reach **~1.1 million by 2050**. ([BoiseDev](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/04/24/ada-canyon-population-2025/), [KTVB](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/idaho-press/when-will-treasure-valleys-explosive-growth-slow-population-ada-canyon/277-25df7e3c-8e37-4ef1-aa19-08425a177891))

**What it means:** the acquisition problem is less "convince people to buy" and more "be the first credible local result they find." This rewards local SEO, Google Business Profile, and a clean local website.

### Demographics that matter for local commerce

- **Median household income (Meridian): ~$100,795 (2024)** — well above the U.S. median; Meridian skews affluent and family-oriented. Surrounding cities are more value-conscious: **Nampa ~$74,300**, **Caldwell ~$66,700**. **Eagle is the high end** (older, wealthier, median age ~47.6). ([Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/meridian-id/), [Point2Homes – Eagle](https://www.point2homes.com/US/Neighborhood/ID/Eagle-Demographics.html))
- **Homeownership (Meridian): 74.6%** (vs. ~65% national), **median home value ~$531,600**. High ownership plus high values means homeowners who invest in their spaces — the core furniture/home-services customer. ([Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/meridian-id/))
- **Age mix (Meridian): median ~37.5**, family-heavy; ~28% are 25–44 (peak household-formation and furniture-buying years). ([idaho-demographics.com](https://www.idaho-demographics.com/meridian-demographics))
- **New construction is unusually high.** In 2025, new homes were **~44.5% of Ada and ~49.0% of Canyon County** monthly inventory — Idaho led the nation. YTD median sale price: **$555,000 (Ada)**, **$425,000 (Canyon)**. ([The Agency Boise](https://www.theagency-boise.com/blog/2025-year-end-market-report), [Idaho Business Review](https://idahobusinessreview.com/2025/02/17/treasure-valley-home-prices-new-construction-2025/))

**What it means:** a near-record share of buyers are moving into empty new-builds — a structural tailwind for furniture, window coverings, flooring, cleaning, and moving. **Target Meridian/Eagle for higher price points and Nampa/Caldwell for value positioning.**

### Local business climate

- **No general city business license in Meridian** — only specific activities (alcohol, mobile vending) need a permit. ([City of Meridian](https://meridiancity.org/city-clerk/license-and-permit-applications/))
- **Home-based businesses** are allowed via a **Home Occupation / Accessory Use application** (Meridian UDC §11-4-3.21): at most one non-resident employee, no significant traffic/noise/odor, and code historically restricts on-site sales to products *made* at the home — so a home-based resale/fulfillment setup must be structured carefully. **Confirm current rules with Meridian Planning before relying on this.** ([Idaho SBDC](https://idahosbdc.org/sbdc_faq/i-plan-to-start-my-business-in-my-home-are-there-special-regulations-i-need-to-know-about/), [Meridian Planning FAQ](https://meridiancity.org/community-development/planning/general-information-faq/))
- **Sales tax: a flat 6%.** Idaho's rate is 6%; Meridian, Boise, Eagle, Nampa, and Caldwell add **0% local-option tax** — one rate, corridor-wide, easy to administer. ([Idaho State Tax Commission](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/sales-tax/local-sales-tax/city-sales-tax/), [SalesTaxHandbook](https://www.salestaxhandbook.com/idaho))

The full legal/entity/tax setup — LLC, EIN, bank account, seller's permit, resale certificate, and remittance — is in **Playbook D.0** below.

### Seasonality → a concrete promo calendar

The region runs a roughly three-cycle calendar: **outdoor/recreation peak May–Sept**; **late Aug–Nov** layering BSU football and holiday retail; and a **post-holiday slowdown Jan–March** with the thinnest walk-in traffic. ([idaho@work](https://idahoatwork.com/2024/11/15/idaho-retail-industry-navigates-fast-approaching-holiday-season/)) For a furniture/home business the buying signal tracks the **move-in / new-construction calendar** (peaking late spring through early fall) more than tourism. Convert that into a standing offer plan:

| Window | Demand state | Standing offer / focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Mar–May** | Moving/nesting ramps up | "Free local delivery" + new-home bundles; raise ad budget |
| **Jun–Aug** | Peak move-in + events (Alive After 5, farmers markets, Boise Music Festival) | Inventory buys heaviest; event/partnership presence; financing push |
| **Sep–Nov** | Football + holiday run-up | Gift/holiday angles; second budget peak; referral drive |
| **Dec** | Holiday tail | Clear slow movers; gift cards |
| **Jan–Mar** | Slowest walk-in | Clearance/discount season; relationship-building, review/referral catch-up, content |

### Where the gap is — and the reusable implication

- **Furniture resale: 100% have NO online checkout.** Services have largely closed the gap (Top Shelf, Gemstar, Junkluggers lead on reviews *because* they removed friction).

## Local demand & channel map

A constant stream of newcomers who have no "my guy" yet is the most important fact for any local operator. Year-over-year (2025→2026), **Caldwell (+5,160), Kuna (+4,810), and Meridian (+4,730)** gained the most people. The buyer who searches "sectional couch boise" today may have moved here three months ago. ([Idaho Business Review](https://idahobusinessreview.com/2026/05/18/treasure-valley-population-grows-150000-six-years/))

### The five ways locals actually discover businesses

1. **Google Maps / the local "3-pack."** The local pack appears in ~93% of local searches and captures ~44% of clicks; ~68% trust the 3-pack most. If you're not in it for your core terms, you're invisible to nearly half your demand. ([RedLocal](https://redlocalagency.com/google-local-pack-statistics/), [Keystar](https://www.keystaragency.com/google-maps-statistics/))
2. **"Near me" / high-intent search.** ~46% of Google searches have local intent; ~76% of nearby searchers visit a business within 24 hours and ~28% of "near me" searches end in a purchase. ([BiziQ](https://biziq.com/blog/local-search-statistics/), [Map Labs](https://maplabs.com/top-local-search-statistics-2025/))
3. **Facebook Marketplace + local Buy/Sell/Trade groups** — arguably the dominant discovery channel for physical goods here, and free. ([Treasure Valley BST](https://www.facebook.com/groups/bstid/), [Marketplace Boise](https://www.facebook.com/marketplace/boise/))
4. **Nextdoor** — ~46M weekly users; 94% trust peer recommendations; pages with 5+ recommendations get ~30% more engagement. ([Nextdoor](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/small-business/resources/blog/new-nextdoor-insights-show-neighbors-strongly-support-local-businesses))
5. **Reviews as digital word-of-mouth** — ~87% read reviews before choosing a local business. ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/))

**Buyer behavior that decides outcomes:** reply fast (see the standardized rule below), advertise **free local delivery in the Treasure Valley** (a 60-mile radius from one location covers the whole corridor), and surface **financing** ("as low as $X/mo" / "no-credit") directly in listings — ~72% of Americans plan to use BNPL, and furniture/home décor is ~26% of BNPL-financed categories. ([Numerator](https://www.numerator.com/resources/blog/buy-now-pay-later-market-insights/))

> ### Two standardized rules used everywhere in this playbook
>
> **Lead-response rule:** **Reply within 5 minutes during business hours; auto-acknowledge instantly when you can't.** Responding within 5 minutes vs. an hour makes you ~7x more likely to qualify a lead; ~78% of customers buy from the *first* business that responds. Missed-call-text-back (Playbook E) makes this automatic. ([Teamgate/HBR](https://www.teamgate.com/blog/lead-response-time-study-speed-impacts-revenue/), [Voiso](https://voiso.com/articles/lead-response-time-metrics/))
>
> **Review-volume rule:** **Clear a credibility floor of ~10–20 reviews at 4.5★, then beat your top-3 map competitors' count, maintaining 3+ new reviews/month indefinitely.** Cadence and recency beat raw count (~73% of consumers only trust reviews from the last 30 days). Aim for a **4.2–4.5★** "trust sweet spot" and respond to every review within 24–48 hours. ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/), [Shapo](https://shapo.io/blog/google-review-statistics/))

## Playbook A — Get found for free: local SEO & Google Business Profile

Google ranks local results on **relevance, distance, and prominence**. You can't change distance, so the work goes into relevance (categories, content) and prominence (reviews, citations, links, completeness). GBP completeness drives ~32% of local-pack ranking power; reviews ~20%. ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors/), [PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/local-seo-ranking-factors-2025/))

### Part 1 — Set up and fully optimize your GBP

1. **Claim and verify** at business.google.com (video verification is now common — have inventory/vehicle ready to film). Nothing ranks until verified.
2. **Storefront vs. service-area (SAB).** Deliver to homes? Set up as an SAB, **hide the street address**, and define service areas. Never list a fake/mailbox address — it's a suspension risk.
3. **Service areas by city name** (up to 20): Meridian, Boise, Eagle, Nampa, Caldwell (+ Kuna, Star, Garden City if you serve them). Only list cities you'll actually travel to.
4. **Categories — your #1 relevance lever.** 1 primary + up to 9 secondary. Pick the *most specific* primary (e.g., "Used furniture store"); secondaries like "Sofa store," "Delivery service." Every category must have matching website content. Browse the full ~4,000-category list at [daltonluka.com](https://daltonluka.com/blog/google-my-business-categories).
5. **Complete 100% of fields.** Real name only (no keyword stuffing — it's a suspension risk), one consistent 208 phone, hours incl. holidays, website, booking link, 750-char description with city names once or twice, attributes.
6. **Photos** — logo, cover, 10–20 real (not stock) shots; add **2–3 new per week** (recency signal).
7. **Products** — add each with photo, price, link to the matching site page.
8. **Services** (service businesses) — list each with a short description.
9. **Messaging on + seed Q&A** — answer 5–8 real questions ("Do you deliver to Nampa?").
10. **Post weekly** via Google Posts (new inventory, an offer, "just delivered in Eagle").

### Part 2 — Get into the local 3-pack

11. **Lock NAP consistency** — Name, Address, Phone byte-for-byte identical everywhere. Consistent NAP makes a business ~40% more likely to appear in the local pack. Write your canonical NAP once and never deviate.
12. **Build citations — local first.** Big aggregators (Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, Facebook, BBB, Nextdoor), then local: **Meridian Chamber** ([directory](https://business.meridianchamber.org/list)), **Boise Metro Chamber**, Nampa/Caldwell chambers, City of Meridian business resources. Find gaps free with Whitespark's Local Citation Finder.
13. **Earn local links** — chamber pages, a local-blog mention, a cross-link with a complementary TV business. A few genuine local links beat dozens of directory links.
14. **Review velocity & response cadence** — apply the standardized review rule above; respond within 24–48 hours.

### Part 3 — On-site local SEO

15. **One genuinely different page per city** (`/sectional-delivery-meridian`, `/boise`, `/nampa`…) with unique copy and real local photos — never near-duplicate "doorway" pages.
16. **On-page basics** — title/H1 with service + city, NAP in footer sitewide, embedded Google Map, internal links between city pages.
17. **Schema** — JSON-LD LocalBusiness (or specific subtype) + Product schema with AggregateRating on product pages. Generate free with Localo's generator; always validate in Google's Rich Results Test.
18. **Mobile speed** — run PageSpeed Insights, serve WebP, lazy-load images, minimize scripts.

### Part 4 — A review-generation system

19. **One canonical review link + QR code** (from GBP "Ask for reviews," or Whitespark's free generator). Put the QR on receipts, packaging, delivery paperwork, email signature, and the site.
20. **Automate the post-purchase ask (SMS + email)** ~1–2 hours after delivery; SMS open rates run ~98%. A face-to-face/QR ask *plus* SMS follow-up converts ~3–4× either alone.
    - *Template:* "Hi [Name], thanks for choosing Boise Sectionals! If you love your new sectional, a quick Google review really helps our small local shop: [link]. Thank you!"
21. **Realistic volume:** 3–8 reviews/month is a credible solo target. **Never gate, buy, or incentivize reviews** (Google-policy violations) — ask *every* customer.

## Playbook B — Pay to accelerate: Google & Meta ads

Built for ~$300–$1,000/month. **Product business (sectionals): Meta-first, Search-second, no Local Services Ads** (LSA is restricted to ~70–100 *service* categories — retail isn't eligible). **Service business: LSA + Search first, Meta for retargeting.**

### B.1 — Foundation before spending a dollar

Tracking is non-negotiable. Install the **Google tag + GA4** (mark `purchase`/qualified-lead as the *primary* conversion, send order value) and the **Meta pixel + Conversions API** (rank events with `Purchase` on top, verify your domain). Use your platform's native integration. Run normal traffic 1–2 weeks and confirm a test order fires events in both before trusting any cost number.

### B.2 — Local Services Ads (service businesses only)

Pay-per-lead, above search ads, "Google Guaranteed" badge. U.S. average ≈ **$60/lead** (range $5–$130+; home services $30–$80; the Treasure Valley is lower-competition, so expect the low end). Complete background/license/insurance verification, set service area to your cities, **reply fast, and dispute junk leads weekly** (free credits). Track lead→booked-job close rate yourself for true cost-per-job. ([WordStream LSA](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/google-local-services-ads))

### B.3 — Google Search Ads (high-intent capture)

Start **$10–$15/day**. 2025 average CPC ~$4.51–$5.26 (Home & Home Improvement ~$7.85; retail lower). Structure: one campaign, 2–3 tight ad groups, **phrase + exact** match, aggressive negatives (`free`, `repair`, `rental`, `jobs`), review the Search Terms report weekly. Geo: **~20–25 mi radius around 83642**, "Presence: people in your targeted locations." Ad angles: name the city, lead with savings, local delivery, trust ("Inspected & refurbished, 30-day guarantee"). All-industry CPL ~$70; beat it with a defined product.

### B.4 — Performance Max (only at scale)

Excellent for e-commerce with a Merchant Center product feed, **but hungry** — don't run it under ~$1,000/mo; it wants 20–30+ conversions/month and ~$50/day. Add account-level negatives + brand exclusions so it doesn't just harvest branded searches. **Don't start here.**

### B.5 — Meta Ads (primary channel for product)

Start **$10–$15/day**. Meta is cheaper per click (FB CPC ~$1.06, CPM ~$6.59–$7.47; IG Feed CPC ~$3.35) and the cheapest place to retarget. Lead-gen averaged ~7.72% conv. and ~$27.66 CPL in 2025. Two campaigns: **Prospecting** (Advantage+ broad, geo-capped to the corridor; start Traffic/Engagement to seed the pixel, switch to Sales) and **Retargeting**. Creative wins or loses it — **before/after refurb reels** are the single best format for resale furniture; run 3–5 creatives, kill losers weekly, refresh every 2–4 weeks. Average Meta ROAS ~2.79; aim higher on high-margin items.

### B.6 — Retargeting (highest-ROI spend)

Almost nobody buys a $1,500 sofa on first visit. Meta: dynamic product ads to site visitors (30d), cart/view-content abandoners (14d), 75% video viewers; build a Lookalike off purchasers at ~100 buyers. Google: remarketing lists via Display/PMax once budget allows. Exclude recent purchasers from prospecting.

### B.7 — The $300/month starter plan

| When | Action | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| Week 0 | Install Google tag + GA4 + Meta pixel + CAPI; define `Purchase` primary; verify test order. Confirm not LSA-eligible (retail). | $0 |
| Weeks 1–4 | **Meta prospecting ~$7/day** (~$210/mo): Advantage+ broad, 20–25 mi radius, before/after reel-led | ~$210/mo |
| Weeks 1–4 | **Google Search ~$3/day** (~$90/mo): one campaign, 2 ad groups, phrase+exact long-tail local terms, aggressive negatives | ~$90/mo |
| Ongoing | Weekly: add negatives, kill losing creatives, check cost-per-purchase; pause anything 2–3× over target CPA | — |
| Proven | Carve **$3/day for retargeting** (usually best ROAS) | reallocate |

**Scaling rules:** change budgets at most weekly in ~20% steps; only scale a channel hitting your target cost-per-sale (max CPA = gross margin × acceptable %); add PMax only past ~$1,000–$1,500/mo with a feed and 20–30 monthly conversions.

## Playbook C — Compounding & free: Marketplace, community & referrals

The "time, not money" engine. **Win the local discovery surfaces for free, prove yourself with reviews/referrals, and funnel every contact to a channel you own (your site + an SMS list).**

### C1. Facebook Marketplace — primary local sales engine

- **Photos first (up to 10):** clean hero shot, every angle, honest flaws, a tape-measure dimension shot.
- **Keyword-rich ~65-char title:** `Refurbished Leather Sectional — Designer, Like New (Meridian)` (brand + condition + category + city).
- **Description = the FAQ you're tired of answering** + one funnel line ("More inventory + delivery at boise-sectionals.com"). Put the link in the description, not the title.
- **Price 5–10% above your floor** (buyers haggle).
- **Refresh every ~7 days**; delete-and-relist stale items with a new hero photo; mark **Sold** immediately.
- **Respond per the 5-minute rule** with a saved reply; the first responsive seller usually wins.
- **Funnel off-platform** — Marketplace is rented land. Capture phone numbers for an SMS list (see compliance in E).

### C2. Local Facebook buy/sell groups

Large active groups exist (e.g., a Meridian-area BST reported ~36,000–49,000 members — *verify counts inside each group; pages are login-gated*). Read pinned rules, cross-post into 2–4 relevant groups *spaced out*, and answer "where can I buy…?" threads helpfully rather than blasting listings.

### C3. Nextdoor — neighborhood trust

Claim a free Business Page; recommendations are the asset (pages with recommendations get up to ~19× the views). Free business posts reach ~2 miles, twice/month — use them deliberately. Over-indexes on homeowners — the exact audience furnishing homes.

### C4. Short-form video — Reels & TikTok

Reels reach (~30.8%) far outpaces carousels/static. **Before/after refurbishment** is the single best format; "just in" walkthroughs create urgency; process clips build trust. Hook in the first 3 seconds (open on the "after" or price reveal), localize captions ("Meridian/Boise — local pickup or delivery"), post **3–4/week**, end every clip with the same CTA (site + DM).

### C5. Referral program — turn one buyer into three

Double-sided and symmetric: *"Refer a friend — they get $50 off, you get $50 when they buy."* Make the incentive feel real ($50 each side on a high-ticket sofa), remove friction (QR on the delivery receipt / textable link), and **ask at the peak moment** — right after delivery, handing over two referral cards.

### C6. The review flywheel

Apply the standardized review rule. Set up the GBP review link, ask every customer at the moment of delight, make it a checklist item (not a mood), and recycle great reviews into Reels/listings as social proof.

### C7. Weekly operating rhythm (solo)

| Cadence | Task |
|---|---|
| Daily | Reply to all DMs per 5-min rule; one review ask per completed sale |
| 3–4×/week | Post a Reel/TikTok (before/after, new inventory) |
| Weekly | Renew all Marketplace listings; relist stale with new hero photo; one helpful post/comment in 2–4 groups |
| 2×/month | Free Nextdoor business post |

## Playbook D — The buy button: legal foundation + self-serve checkout

The goal: a customer lands on a page, sees price + financing + delivery, and pays — **the only human action is the customer clicking "Buy."** Idaho's flat **6% sales tax with no local add-on** means one tax rate corridor-wide.

### D.0 — Legal foundation (do this before the buy button)

A one-page setup sequence:

| Step | What | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Form an Idaho LLC** via the Secretary of State | **$100** | LLC separates personal assets from business liability; sole prop is simpler but offers no protection |
| 2 | **Get an EIN** from the IRS | **Free** | Needed for the bank account, payroll, and to avoid using your SSN |
| 3 | **Open a business bank account** | $0–$15/mo | Keep business and personal money separate (required for the LLC liability shield to hold) |
| 4 | **Register for a seller's permit** via Idaho Business Registration (IBR) at the Tax Commission | Free | Lets you collect the 6% sales tax |
| 5 | **File & remit Idaho sales tax** on the **Taxpayer Access Point (TAP)** | — | On your **assigned schedule** (monthly/quarterly/annual, set by the state based on volume). Don't miss the cadence — late filing means penalties |
| 6 | **Resale certificate (Form ST-101)** | Free | Lets you buy inventory for resale **tax-free**; you only charge the 6% on the final retail sale. Give a completed ST-101 to suppliers |
| 7 | **Insurance: general liability + commercial auto** | varies — get quotes | **Required before in-home deliveries.** Personal auto policies *exclude* business delivery; delivering heavy sectionals into homes carries real property/injury liability |
| 8 | **Home-occupation / zoning** | — | If running inventory from home, confirm Meridian UDC §11-4-3.21 rules with Planning before scaling |

### D.0b — Returns / warranty / as-is policy (publish at checkout)

Used-goods sales legally need explicit condition disclosure. Publish a written policy at checkout covering: **condition/"as-is" disclosure** per item, a stated **return window** (e.g., the referenced "30-day guarantee"), what's covered vs. not, and who pays return delivery. This is both a legal requirement and a trust signal.

### D.0c — Pricing & sourcing rule

A resale operator needs a method, not a guess:
- **Source at ≤30–40% of refurbished retail.** If a refurbished sectional sells for $1,400, buy the piece for roughly $420–$560.
- **Price to a target gross margin** (~40%+), benchmarked against **Couchy's $400–$1,600 band** — sit credibly within or just above it for "designer."
- Run a **monthly competitor price-monitoring loop**: check Couchy, Consign Furniture, and Marketplace comps; adjust your bands so you're never the obvious high outlier on identical condition.

### D.0d — Designer niche: authentication & refurb standard

The "designer" premium must be defensible:
- **Verify brand/model** before buying (tags, frame stamps, known designs); don't claim a brand you can't confirm.
- **Document a repeatable refurb standard** — inspect → deep-clean → repair → photograph before/after. Keep the before/after for Marketplace, ads, and reviews.
- **Photograph the process** so the value-add is visible — this is what justifies the premium and earns review-worthy trust.

### 1. Storefront / checkout platform

| Platform | Monthly | Card processing | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shopify Basic** ✅ | $39 ($29 annual) | 2.9% + $0.30 | Delivery + financing handled natively |
| Square Online Free | $0 | 3.3% + $0.30 | Also sell in person, low volume |
| Square Online Plus | $49 | 2.9% + $0.30 | Higher online volume on Square |
| WooCommerce | ~$11–$40 hosting | 2.9% + $0.30 | Full control, you run WordPress |
| Stripe Payment Links | $0 | 2.9% + $0.30 | Tiny static catalog; no native delivery zones/financing menu |

### 2. Financing (the conversion lever)

Card processing is ~identical everywhere; **financing decides big-ticket sales.** Offer two tiers:
- **Prime/near-prime:** Shop Pay Installments (one Shopify toggle, **5.9% + $0.30**) or Affirm (~2.4–6% negotiated).
- **No/low-credit lease-to-own:** **Snap Finance** — the option resale-furniture buyers ask for by name (~80% approval claimed; merchant rate **not publicly disclosed** — get a quote via the merchant portal).

On a $1,500 sale, Shop Pay Installments costs ~$88.80 vs. ~$43.80 for plain card — that ~$45 delta is cheap if it converts a buyer who'd otherwise walk.

### 3. Selling a SERVICE self-serve (reusable pattern)

**Productize each service into a flat-price "Book Now" page, take a deposit at booking, let the calendar schedule it.** Use **Square Appointments (Free)** or **Acuity Starter ($20/mo)**; require a 25–50% deposit and keep a card on file for no-shows.

### 4. Local logistics inside checkout (the Treasure Valley part)

Shopify native local delivery handles the basics free: **up to 10 zones per location** by **radius (≤100 mi)** or **zip list**, each with its own price — e.g., **$0 to Meridian/Eagle, $39 to Nampa/Caldwell**, with a free-delivery threshold. Offer pickup/curbside for anyone outside your radius. For true zip-tiered pricing + **scheduled delivery windows**, add **Zapiet – Rates by Zip/Distance** (worth it for bulky sectionals on set days).

### 4b. Hold / deposit / no-show + delivery logistics

- **Hold rule:** a **non-refundable deposit holds an item 48 hours**; no deposit, no hold. Forfeited deposits cover the opportunity cost of the item sitting off-market.
- **No-show rule:** card on file; charge a stated fee or forfeit the deposit on no-show.
- **Route logistics:** **batch deliveries by city/day** (e.g., Nampa/Caldwell Tuesdays, Meridian/Eagle Thursdays) to cut windshield time — critical for a solo operator running one van.
- **Post-delivery handoff:** confirm condition with the customer at drop-off, hand over the returns/warranty card + two referral cards, and trigger the review request (Playbook E, Scenario 6).

### Recommendation

**Product:** Shopify Basic + Shopify Payments + Shop Pay Installments + Snap Finance + native delivery zones (+ Zapiet for scheduled windows). **Service:** Square Appointments (Free) or Acuity Starter, deposit at booking, card on file.

## Playbook E — Hands-off backend: the automation backbone

**One inbox, one calendar, one automation hub.** Two 2026 constraints shape the design: **GBP chat is dead** (removed July 31, 2024 — capture via the call button + a form on your linked page), and **Marketplace can't be fully API-automated** (use Meta's built-in AI auto-reply for first touch, then pull serious buyers off-platform to your phone/booking link). Both highest-volume channels funnel to a **phone number + website link**, so build a phone-centric spine.

### The automated flow

```
INBOUND: Website form · Marketplace msg · GBP "Call" · Missed call/voicemail
   ↓ (all land in)
(1) ONE INBOX / CRM
   ↓
(2) INSTANT AUTO-RESPONSE (<1 min, 24/7): missed-call-text-back + form autoresponder + AI triage draft
   ↓
(3) QUALIFY (2–3 Qs): in-area? budget? timeline? → HOT (booking link) / NURTURE (drip) / JUNK (tag & drop)
   ↓
(4) BOOK / CONFIRM: self-serve calendar slot → auto SMS+email confirmation + Square receipt → Google Calendar event
   ↓
(5) REMINDERS: T-24h SMS, T-2h "on the way" SMS
   ↓
(6) FULFILLMENT HANDOFF: delivery status updates + internal checklist
   ↓
(7) POST-SALE: +1 day review request (direct Google link) · +14 day referral ask · tag customer → remarketing
```

### The glue: which platform

Two sane stacks:

**Stack A — "Cheap, assembled" (~$25–35/mo fixed) ✅ default**

| Role | Tool | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Phone inbox + missed-call-text-back + SMS | OpenPhone/Quo | $15/mo |
| Booking + confirmations + receipts + reminders | Square Appointments (free) + POS | $0 (+ processing) |
| Contact database + form + basic email | HubSpot Free (or Google Sheet) | $0 |
| The glue (form→CRM→SMS→calendar→review) | Make.com Core | ~$10/mo |
| Calendar of record | Google Calendar | $0 |
| AI triage / reply drafting | Claude via Make/n8n | a few $/mo usage |

**Stack B — "One tool, hands-off" (~$110–140/mo all-in):** GoHighLevel Starter ($97/mo) bundles CRM + inbox + SMS/email + calendar + missed-call-text-back + review requests. Choose it past ~15 leads/day or when juggling integrations costs more than $70/mo. GHL's missed-call-text-back recovers 30–60% of unanswered calls.

### Build order (no-code, a weekend)

1. **Scenario 2 first — missed-call-text-back** (highest ROI): missed call → SMS within 60s ("Sorry I missed you — which sectional, and what's your zip?") → create CRM contact. **Pays for the whole stack with one recovered sale.**
2. **Scenario 1 — website form** → create contact + instant SMS/email + tag.
3. **Scenario 3 — AI triage:** pass the thread to Claude with a prompt that extracts zip/in-service-area/budget/timeline and classifies HOT/NURTURE/JUNK. **Keep a human in the loop for the first 2–4 weeks** — have Claude *draft*, you approve with one tap; then auto-send the safe branches. Give Claude the Calendar tool so it proposes real open windows ("Nampa Thursday 2–4pm or Saturday morning?").
4. **Scenario 4 — booking → confirmation + Google Calendar event + Square receipt.**
5. **Scenario 5 — reminders** (T-24h, T-2h "on the way").
6. **Scenario 6 — post-sale review request** (+1 day, direct Google link) + referral (+14 days).

### SMS / email compliance (required)

Any SMS list needs **explicit opt-in**, **A2P 10DLC registration** of your sending number, and **"Reply STOP to opt out"** in automated texts (plus honoring opt-outs immediately). Email needs a clear unsubscribe. Build consent capture into your form and at point of sale — don't text people who didn't opt in.

### Platform-ban contingency

Marketplace/social account loss is a named risk. Mitigate: **own your funnel** (your site + opted-in SMS/email list is the asset no platform can revoke), keep listings exportable, and don't run unofficial Marketplace "bots" (terms violation → ban). If an account is lost, you still reach your customer list directly.

### Reusability

Swap the qualifying questions and the service-area check; everything else (one inbox, missed-call-text-back, booking, reminders, review request) is reusable verbatim — lawn care, detailing, HVAC, bakery custom orders.

## Playbook F — Know what works: metrics & funnel tracking

One question every week: *where is money going in, and where are sales coming out?*

### The funnel: four stages

```
IMPRESSION → CLICK → LEAD → SALE
(seen you)  (visited) (raised  (paid)
                       a hand)
```

- Low **impressions** → visibility problem (budget/SEO/GBP).
- Clicks but few **leads** → website/offer problem (no clear contact, prices scare, weak photos).
- Leads but few **sales** → follow-up/closing problem (slow replies, price/terms lose).

Find the *first* stage that dropped — that's your leak.

### The six numbers that matter

1. **CPL** = spend ÷ leads. Benchmarks: all-industry ~$70; furniture ~$121.51; Meta lead ads ~$27.66 (colder).
2. **CPA/CAC** = spend ÷ *paying customers*. **Must be comfortably below gross profit per order.**
3. **Conversion rate** at click→lead and lead→sale. Furniture click→lead ~2.73%; e-comm site-wide ~1.2–1.8%. Lead→sale is yours to learn (track 60 days).
4. **AOV** = revenue ÷ orders. Furniture e-comm category avg ~$546 — yours likely higher, which makes a high CPL survivable.
5. **ROAS** = revenue ÷ spend. Uses *revenue, not profit* — know your margin. All-industry ~3.7; search ~5:1; Meta ~1.93; tight local 2x+.
6. **Lead response time** — apply the 5-minute rule; it's the cheapest edge you have.

### Free tool stack ($0)

GBP Performance tab (calls/clicks/directions) · GA4 (mark form/call/checkout/purchase as Key Events) · Google Tag/GTM · Meta Pixel (+CAPI if running Meta) · **a Google Sheet** (the one place all six numbers + offline phone sales live — GA4 will undercount off-site closes). Always log **"how did you hear about us?"** per sale.

### UTM tagging

Append `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign` to every link *you* place (social bios, email, SMS, QR on flyers). **Always lowercase + hyphens; never tag internal links; keep a naming key.** Google Ads auto-tags itself. Read results in GA4 → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition.

### The weekly 15-minute Monday ritual

Scoreboard sheet, one row per channel per week: `Week | Channel | Spend | Leads | CPL | Sales | Revenue | CPA | Conv% | ROAS | Avg response time | One action`.

1. (3 min) Pull raw numbers. 2. (4 min) Fill the row. 3. (3 min) Compare to last week + break-even. 4. (3 min) Walk the funnel top-down, find the leak. 5. (2 min) Write **ONE action**.

**Rules:** one action per week; don't kill a channel on one bad week (judge CPA/ROAS on a 4-week rolling window for high-ticket); always log the attribution question.

## Caveats & verify-before-you-spend

- **Benchmarks are ceilings, not targets.** Every CPL/CPA/ROAS/conversion figure is a 2025–2026 third-party average — your offer, geography, and season can move them 30–50%. Your own trailing numbers always beat a national average.
- **Pull your own keyword volume.** Treat any quoted search count with suspicion; use Keyword Planner/Trends against a live account.
- **Verify before relying on:** Meridian home-occupation/zoning rules (call Planning); current chamber dues; Snap/Affirm/Klarna merchant rates (negotiated, not published); Zapiet app pricing; Idaho LLC fee and TAP filing cadence (confirm with the Tax Commission).
- **Algorithm-weight percentages** (GBP ~32%, reviews ~20%) are third-party analyses, not official Google figures.
- **Platforms change.** Marketplace can't be fully bot-automated and accounts can be lost — own your funnel (site + opted-in list). GBP chat is gone; capture via call + form.
- **Legal/insurance are not optional.** Don't do in-home deliveries on a personal auto policy; don't sell used goods without an as-is/returns disclosure; don't run an SMS list without opt-in + A2P 10DLC + STOP.
- **Review integrity:** never gate, buy, or incentivize reviews — it violates Google policy and risks removal.
